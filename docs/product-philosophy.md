# Product Philosophy

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Living Document
**Last Updated:** 2026-07-28

---

# Why this document exists

Features, technologies and roadmaps will change over time.

This philosophy should not.

It defines the principles behind every decision we make while building Nomad Guide.

Whenever we are unsure whether a feature belongs in the project, this document should help us answer one simple question:

> **Does this improve the travel experience?**

If not, we should carefully reconsider whether it belongs in Nomad Guide.

---

# Built by travellers, for travellers.

Nomad Guide is created from real travel experiences.

The ideas behind the project are not invented in a meeting room—they originate from real journeys, real challenges and real adventures.

The project grows together with every new destination.

---

# Traveller First

Every important decision should create value for the traveller.

Technology, architecture and implementation are important, but they always serve the user—not the other way around.

Whenever we must choose between technical elegance and a significantly better user experience, we should carefully evaluate whether the additional complexity is truly justified.

---

# The traveller always decides

Nomad Guide is a companion.

Never a guide that dictates decisions.

The application may recommend, explain and inspire.

The final decision always belongs to the traveller.

Freedom is one of the most valuable parts of independent travel and should never be reduced by the software.

---

# Encourage Discovery

The best travel experiences are often unplanned.

Nomad Guide should encourage curiosity instead of replacing it.

The application should help travellers discover new places, activities and opportunities without trying to optimise every moment of their journey.

Sometimes the unexpected becomes the most memorable experience.

---

# Learn from journeys, not assumptions

Every meaningful feature should solve a real travel problem.

Ideas should come from real experiences whenever possible.

Observations made while travelling are more valuable than assumptions made behind a desk.

Real journeys are the project's best source of inspiration.

---

# Understand before suggesting

Nomad Guide should gradually understand the traveller.

Not through complicated questionnaires.

But through experiences, preferences and previous journeys.

The goal is not to predict every decision.

The goal is to provide increasingly relevant suggestions while preserving freedom of choice.

The application should adapt to the traveller.

The traveller should never have to adapt to the application.

---

# Technology should disappear behind the experience

Travellers should not notice how something works.

They should simply feel that it works naturally.

Artificial Intelligence, databases and algorithms are tools.

They are never the product itself.

The best technology is almost invisible.

---

# Simplicity is a feature

Travelling is already complex.

Managing a journey should not be.

Every screen, workflow and feature should strive to reduce complexity instead of adding more.

Simple solutions are usually better than clever ones.

---

# Respect the journey

Not every minute needs optimisation.

Not every recommendation needs to be followed.

Sometimes getting lost creates the best memories.

Nomad Guide should support the journey—not control it.

---

# One place for what matters

Travellers often switch between maps, notes, bookings, expenses, photos, chat conversations and travel guides.

Nomad Guide aims to bring together the information that matters most while remaining focused and easy to use.

The goal is not to replace every travel application.

The goal is to become the place travellers naturally return to throughout their journey.

---

# Grow through craftsmanship

Nomad Guide is also a learning project.

The goal is not only to build useful software.

The goal is to continuously improve as software engineers.

Good documentation, thoughtful architecture, clean code and well-reasoned decisions are considered features of the project itself.

---

# AI Philosophy

Artificial Intelligence should support travellers—not replace their curiosity.

AI should help people:

* discover opportunities
* organise information
* recognise patterns
* remember experiences
* make informed decisions

AI should never remove the joy of exploration.

Its purpose is to empower people, not to make decisions for them.

---

# Decision Filter

Before implementing any significant feature, ask:

1. Does this solve a real travel problem?
2. Does it create meaningful value for the traveller?
3. Does it respect the traveller's freedom?
4. Is it simple enough?
5. Does it align with our long-term vision?
6. Is this the right time to build it? (YAGNI)

If several answers are "No", the feature probably does not belong in the current version.

---

# Closing Thought

Nomad Guide is not about travelling more.

It is about travelling better.

Not according to someone else's definition of the perfect journey—

but according to each traveller's own way of exploring the world.
